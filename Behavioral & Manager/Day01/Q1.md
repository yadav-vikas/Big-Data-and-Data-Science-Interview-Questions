Question : Tell me about a time when you strongly disagreed with a technical decision made by someone on your team or another team. What happened, what did you do, and what was the outcome?

answer : "When I disagree with someone's technical approach, I first try to understand their reasoning rather than immediately defending my own solution. I break the discussion into what we agree on and where our assumptions differ.

For the areas of disagreement, I try to identify whether we can validate the assumptions using data, experiments, benchmarks, production evidence, or some other objective criteria.

Once we have that evidence, we compare the options based on the problem we're actually trying to solve—things like scalability, reliability, cost, delivery time, and operational complexity.

My goal isn't to prove that my approach is correct. My goal is to make sure we choose the approach that covers the most important requirements and risks.

And once a decision is made, even if my approach isn't selected, I support the decision and help the team execute it."
